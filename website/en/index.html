Features

Simple rules that actually stick.

Pick the apps that eat your time and the rule that suits you. Custos takes care of the rest, in the background.

  • A quota per app

    Set a maximum time per day for each app. Once the quota is used up, the app stays blocked until the next day. You can also choose the days and hours when it is allowed.

    Example: 30 minutes of Instagram a day.

  • Curfews

    Block apps during a time window, on the days you pick. Their notifications can be paused at the same time.

    Example: social media blocked from 10 pm to 7 am on weekdays.

  • Short sessions

    Limit how long each opening lasts, enforce a break before the next one and cap the number of openings per day.

    Example: 5 minutes per opening, 3 times a day.

  • Block just part of an app

    With partial access, close only the section that sucks you in and keep the rest of the app. Show Custos the screen to block and the one to send you back to, and it remembers.

    Example: YouTube Shorts, without blocking YouTube.

  • Nuclear Mode

    For exam prep or deep work: block a set of apps for a fixed time, with Do Not Disturb switched on. No way to cancel it early.

    Example: 2 hours without social media before an exam.

How it works

Ready in three steps.

No account to create, no complicated setup. The app walks you through each step.

  1. 1

    Download and install

    Get the APK from the official releases page on GitHub and open it on your phone. Android asks you to allow the install: that is normal for an app installed outside the Play Store.

  2. 2

    Grant the permissions

    Turn on accessibility, so Custos knows which app is open, and usage access, to measure your screen time. The app explains each one.

  3. 3

    Set your rules

    Add your apps and pick a quota, a curfew or a session limit. When a limit is reached, Custos sends you back to the home screen.

Privacy and trust

Custos collects nothing. At all.

Custos does not even ask for internet access. So it cannot send anything: no account, no ads, no analytics. Your rules and your screen time are stored on your phone, and Custos never sends them anywhere.

  • No internet permission

    No server, no tracker, no data collected. You can check it in the app's code.

  • No root, no special privileges

    No root, no device admin mode. Custos installs and uninstalls like any other app.

  • Open source, MIT licence

    All the code is public on GitHub. Read it, build it yourself or suggest improvements.

  • Anti-cheat delay

    Loosening a rule only takes effect after a delay you choose. Tightening it is instant. By the time the delay is over, so is the urge.

  • Always on

    Custos restarts with your phone and relaunches itself if it gets stopped. Your progress for the day is kept.

  • Free, no ads

    No subscription, no in-app purchase, no advertising. Just the app.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Is Custos really free?

Yes. Custos is free and open source, under the MIT licence. There are no ads, no subscription and no in-app purchases.

Does Custos collect my data?

No. The app has no account and does not request the internet permission: it cannot send anything. Your rules and usage time are stored only on your phone, and the history of the apps you open is not recorded. Details in the privacy policy.

Which phones does Custos run on?

Android 8.0 and every newer version. Custos is not available on iPhone: iOS does not let an app watch other apps this way.

Is it safe to install an APK?

Yes, as long as you get it from the source: the official releases page on GitHub. The code is public, and every release is signed with the same key, so an update installs over the previous version without losing your settings.

On the first install, Android asks you to allow your browser or file manager to install apps. You can turn that permission off again right after, in Settings.

Do I need to root my phone?

No. Custos needs no root, no device admin mode and no computer. It relies on the Android accessibility service and installs like a regular app.

Why does Custos need accessibility access?

That is how it knows which app is in the foreground and sends you back to the home screen when a rule applies. It also uses it to protect its own settings. Nothing it sees is sent anywhere, since the app has no internet access.

Can Custos be bypassed?

Custos is a self-discipline tool, not tamper-proof parental control. A determined person can turn the service off in Settings or uninstall the app. Custos guards the sensitive settings pages and puts a delay on every loosening: enough friction to break the habit, and that is the whole point.

When will Custos be on Google Play?

The Google Play release is in preparation. In the meantime, the APK on GitHub is the official version.
